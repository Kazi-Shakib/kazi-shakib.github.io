HDKEM: deniable, hybrid post-quantum key exchange

Manuscript under revision

A hybrid deniable authenticated key exchange that combines ML-KEM-768, X25519, and an optional QKD or pre-shared key under a split-key PRF combiner, with deniability from Gandalf ring signatures. Proving eCK security in the QROM exposed a subtle soundness gap in an earlier design: ring signatures don't hide the signed message. Fixing it led to a capability-allocation taxonomy that classifies how deniable key exchange designs allocate cryptographic witnesses.

  • eCK / QROM proofs
  • ProVerif models
  • Full C implementation
  • End-to-end benchmarks

Authentication that survives key depletion in QKD

Manuscript under revision

An attacker who can't break quantum key distribution can still drain the pre-shared keys its classical channel needs, forcing a denial of service. My Quantum Authentication Reservoir authenticates with CHSH-based spot checks while replenishing key material. A Lyapunov drift proof shows the reservoir doesn't deplete under a bounded adversary, and an M/D/1 queueing analysis gives its availability. I derived the adversary's optimal cheating rate (QBER*) in closed form, and I'm now tightening the finite-key bound with a complementary-basis entropic uncertainty relation.

  • CHSH spot checks
  • Lyapunov stability
  • M/D/1 availability
  • Finite-key analysis

Protocol security for the smart grid

DOE funded

A complete DNP3 (IEEE 1815) protocol stack in C with ECDH/ECDSA mutual authentication and ANSI C12.22 meter data tunneling, plus CATA-KEY, a trust-adaptive key establishment scheme for grid networks. Related work compares classical and post-quantum public-key cryptography for DNP3, and a PKC-integrated ANSI C12.22 design runs 1.58× faster than the plain protocol, validated with official test vectors and differential testing.

Where my research is going

Two directions I'm building toward.

From hand-written proofs to machine-checked ones

ProVerif gave HDKEM useful symbolic guarantees, but it can't tell a classical adversary from a quantum one or certify the computational reduction behind a QROM proof. I want to pair symbolic models of protocol logic with machine-checked computational proofs, in the style of CryptoVerif, so that deniability, key secrecy, and authentication are certified rather than argued by hand.

A composition theory for deniability

I plan to extend my capability-allocation taxonomy into a general theory of how deniability composes, so a deniable handshake layered under an authenticated transport comes with a provable guarantee about what the combination reveals.
